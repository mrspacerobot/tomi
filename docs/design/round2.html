Tomi for iPhone, design round 2

The tasting menu, from first launch to tonight's special

Direction B, taken through the three things asked for: the whole onboarding, home before a fridge scan, and the scan flow, including the saved cupboard. Same palette, type and print-style Tomi as round 1. Onboarding is seven questions between a welcome and two permission primers. Every question has a default or advances on a single tap, so the shortest path through is 11 taps plus the two iOS permission dialogs, about a minute. Only the two questions that decide what is safe to serve cannot be skipped. A skipped question keeps its default and can be answered later in Settings.

Before building

Open questions, and what to cut

Open questions

  • Level names. I used Novice for level 1 and Commis for level 2. The full ladder (Chef de partie, Sous chef, and so on) needs deciding, or the rank column goes back to a plain level number.
  • Does "never" a cuisine hide alternatives too? I assumed yes: never means never, on the special and on "also on tonight".
  • Fish appears twice, as a diet (pescatarian) and as something to avoid. I kept both because one is preference and one is allergy, but a vegan who ticks nothing still needs dairy and eggs excluded by the diet rule, not by this screen.
  • Uncertain ingredients. I show them dashed with a question mark and count them as present unless removed. The safer rule is to leave them out of matching until confirmed. Which one is a product call.
  • Cupboard-only dishes. The "nothing fits" screen promises a dish that needs nothing from the fridge. The lesson library has to contain a few for each first-course technique.
  • Reminder time. 17:30 is a guess. It could be asked, or learned from when the first few scans happen.
  • Not designed this round: the keyboard-up state, the iOS permission dialogs themselves, the denied-camera state, Settings, and dark theme.

If onboarding feels too long, cut in this order

  • 1. Move the notification primer (1j) to the first timer. The reason is most convincing at the moment a timer is about to start. Saves a screen and an iOS dialog.
  • 2. Move the cupboard (1h) to the first scan review. It already appears there. Pre-tick the same staples and let the cook correct them in context.
  • 3. Fold table and units (1g) into defaults. Two people and the phone's region are right for most. Offer the change on the first recipe card.
  • 4. Move cuisines (1d) to after the first cooked lesson. The owner raised this one, so I would keep it until the others are gone, but it is the least needed on day one.
  • Keep to the end: diet, avoid, kitchen, experience, and the camera primer. They change tonight's dish or its safety.

Recommendation

Ship cuts 1 and 2 from the start: nine screens, nine taps, one iOS dialog.

Why. The cupboard and the notification request both work better at the moment they matter, and both already have a home there: the scan review shows the cupboard, and the first timer is the honest time to ask to ring. That leaves six questions, each of which changes what is served tonight, plus the welcome, the camera primer and the done screen.

Keep the cuisines screen. It answers the owner's case directly (Mexican struck off the menu in one tap), costs one tap for people with no strong feelings, and is where the later guest menus get their first mention.

Home before a scan. Keep the blank special. It teaches the layout of the home screen before there is anything in it, and makes the scan result feel like an answer.
